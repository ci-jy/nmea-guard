nmea-guard results

A heap-free C++17 NMEA 0183 parser that rejects garbled GPS sentences and recovers from serial overruns. This page is generated by scripts/build_report.py from the files in results/.

Damaged sentences that got through

A quarter of the clean GGA, RMC, GSA and VTG sentences in 173 gpsd receiver logs were damaged with one fault at a time (seeds 1, 2, 3), and each damaged log was replayed through nmea-guard and through a naive line parser that only checks *hh at the end of a line. A damaged sentence "got through" if the parser accepted it as a position, time or speed.

When only the line end is lost, nmea-guard still decodes the first of the two run-together sentences on its checksum: 8,281 of 8,327 came back, against 296 for the naive parser. Nearly everything that got past nmea-guard had lost bytes or flipped bits that cancel out under XOR and left well-formed fields behind; the details are in corruption-eval.md.

Cortex-M3 build

The size probe is the smallest realistic firmware: it polls the board's UART, feeds every byte to a static Parser and keeps the last valid position. Built with arm-none-eabi-g++ 14.2.1 -Os, newlib-nano, --gc-sections, it needs 8,237 bytes of flash and 1,152 bytes of RAM, plus the stack. No heap is linked in at all.

On QEMU's mps2-an385 board the runner fed 797 test vectors from the gpsd logs (clean sentences, other types, and copies with dropped bytes or flipped bits) to one Parser, byte by byte with CR/LF, and 797 produced the expected event. All 1,262 decoded numbers matched pynmea2, 1,262 of them to the last bit, even though the Cortex-M3 does its double arithmetic in software. 4 of 4 scenarios passed (buffer overflow, reset after an overrun, two sentences run together, a fix built from one epoch).
